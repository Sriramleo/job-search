# Phase 050 — AI-Powered Germany Job Search, Controlled Auto-Apply & Recruiter Outreach Engine

## 1. Mission

Upgrade the existing Germany Job Search Dashboard into a high-activity, AI-assisted job acquisition system that maximizes relevant applications, qualified recruiter conversations, and interview opportunities before the GCP free-trial credit expiry on December 17, 2026.

The system must run two coordinated workflows:

1. **Job Acquisition Agent:** discover, verify, qualify, prioritize, prepare, and—after explicit production authorization and configured safeguards—submit eligible job applications.
2. **Recruiter Outreach Agent:** identify relevant recruiters and hiring managers, research their professional context, create personalized outreach drafts, manage follow-ups, and track replies. Actual email delivery remains disabled until separately authorized and a compliant sending mechanism is configured.

Use AI where it adds measurable value. Do not maximize token usage merely to consume credits. Maximize relevant opportunities and interview conversion per unit of cost.

Begin by inspecting the current implementation and deployment. Do not rebuild functionality that already exists.

## 4. Job Acquisition Agent

### 4.1 Discovery

Expand useful coverage of official German company career sites and supported ATS feeds.

Prioritize roles such as:
- Senior DevOps Engineer.
- Platform Engineer / Senior Platform Engineer.
- Site Reliability Engineer.
- Cloud Infrastructure Engineer.
- Kubernetes Engineer.
- Cloud Platform / Infrastructure Automation Engineer.
- Relevant engineering leadership roles where actual responsibilities match the candidate's evidence.

Prioritize Germany-based roles, including remote positions explicitly available from Germany.

Preserve for every job:
- Canonical job ID and company ID.
- Official source URL and direct application URL.
- Source provider and source job ID.
- Location and work model.
- Posted date, when available.
- Last verified timestamp.
- Required skills and responsibilities.
- Language requirements.
- Salary range, only when published or otherwise reliably evidenced.
- Work authorization and sponsorship statements, if explicitly stated.
- Evidence provenance, unknown fields, and freshness status.

Never invent missing values.

### 4.2 Freshness verification

Use bounded, rate-limited checks against authoritative job pages or ATS APIs.

Classify each result:
- `VERIFIED`: evidence supports that the vacancy remains available.
- `JOB_UNAVAILABLE`: authoritative evidence confirms closure or removal.
- `UNKNOWN`: evidence is inconclusive due to network errors, access restrictions, rate limits, CAPTCHA, or other uncertainty.

Never classify a `403`, `429`, timeout, or CAPTCHA challenge as proof that a vacancy is closed.

Do not bypass authentication, CAPTCHA, MFA, or anti-bot protections.

### 4.3 AI qualification and ranking

Use a structured evaluation pipeline combining deterministic rules with AI analysis.

Evaluate:
- Actual skills overlap.
- Seniority and scope of responsibility.
- AWS, Kubernetes, EKS, Terraform, CI/CD, GitOps, observability, reliability, and automation relevance.
- Production-scale infrastructure experience.
- Role-specific mandatory requirements.
- English and German language requirements.
- Work location and work model.
- Published salary and candidate salary preferences, if configured.
- Work authorization, relocation, and sponsorship requirements.
- Vacancy freshness.
- Evidence completeness.

Output:
- `fit_score` from 0–100.
- Evidence-backed strengths.
- Missing or unverified requirements.
- Hard blockers.
- Confidence and evidence sources.
- Recommended next action.

The AI must explain the score using job-specific evidence. Do not let a high score override mandatory blockers or missing evidence.

Never assume EU Blue Card eligibility means the candidate already has German work authorization.

Use candidate facts only from the verified candidate profile and evidence store.

## 5. Controlled Auto-Apply Engine

Implement a safe, auditable application pipeline that can be enabled deliberately after validation.

### 5.1 Application states

Use the existing canonical lifecycle where possible. Map equivalent states rather than creating competing state machines.

At minimum, distinguish:
- `DISCOVERED`
- `QUALIFIED`
- `READY_FOR_REVIEW`
- `APPROVED_FOR_SUBMISSION`
- `SUBMISSION_IN_PROGRESS`
- `SUBMITTED_CONFIRMED`
- `REJECTED`
- `APPLICATION_BLOCKED`
- `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`

A draft, queued task, or `AUTO_APPLY_READY` status is never proof of a successful submission.

### 5.2 Eligibility gates

Before an application becomes eligible for submission, verify:
- The vacancy is current and relevant.
- The official application URL is available and trusted.
- No duplicate application exists for the same vacancy.
- The candidate profile supports every submitted factual claim.
- Required documents are present and correct.
- Mandatory application questions can be answered truthfully.
- Work authorization and sponsorship questions have verified answers.
- Language requirements and required qualifications have been evaluated.
- No unresolved application attempt exists.
- No policy, security, or website restriction blocks automation.

If any required answer is unknown, route the application to `READY_FOR_REVIEW` or `APPLICATION_BLOCKED`.

Never fabricate salary expectations, years of experience, certifications, education, visa status, language proficiency, personal information, or answers to eligibility questions.

### 5.3 Submission behavior

Start with a dry-run queue and a human review interface.

After tests pass, provide a separate, explicit production-enablement procedure. Do not automatically change `STAGE_0_DRY_RUN`, `is_enabled`, or `auto_apply_enabled` as part of deployment.

The production activation must require explicit authorization and display:
- Submission scope and allowed providers.
- Maximum applications per day.
- Minimum fit threshold.
- Allowed job categories and geography.
- Document generation policy.
- Human review policy.
- Emergency-stop control.
- Expected cost and operational risks.

Initially require approval for each real application. Any later unattended submission mode must be separately authorized and limited to provider-supported workflows that permit automation.

Only submit through legitimate, supported application paths. Do not bypass CAPTCHA, MFA, access restrictions, anti-bot controls, or website terms.

Do not create fictitious accounts, misrepresent the candidate, or submit inaccurate or incomplete answers merely to increase volume.

### 5.4 Submission evidence and retries

Record:
- Application and job IDs.
- Timestamp and provider.
- Submission method.
- Document versions and approved answers.
- Request/attempt identifier.
- Authoritative confirmation evidence.
- Final outcome and audit trail.

Mark `SUBMITTED_CONFIRMED` only after reliable, positive evidence from the ATS or an authoritative confirmation page/message.

If a timeout or uncertain browser outcome occurs:
- Mark `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`.
- Preserve all evidence.
- Block automatic retries.
- Reconcile the status before another attempt.

Use idempotency and atomic reservations to prevent concurrent duplicate submissions.

### 5.5 CV and cover-letter generation

Generate role-specific application documents using only verified candidate facts.

For cover letters:
- Target approximately 250–400 words unless the application specifies otherwise.
- Use concise, natural professional English.
- Address actual responsibilities and requirements.
- Use evidence-backed accomplishments.
- Do not keyword-stuff or copy job descriptions.
- Do not invent German proficiency, work authorization, sponsorship status, or experience.

Keep original and generated documents versioned. Allow the candidate to review changes before submission.

## 6. Recruiter Outreach Agent

Extend the existing Phase 047 outreach system. Do not create a duplicate contact or email subsystem.

### 6.1 Contact discovery

Research relevant:
- Technical recruiters.
- Internal talent acquisition professionals.
- Platform Engineering and Infrastructure hiring managers.
- Engineering managers and SRE leaders.
- Company recruiting teams.

Prioritize people associated with companies that have relevant current vacancies or a credible fit with the candidate's background.

Prefer authoritative company websites and public professional profiles. Record the source URL, role, employer, research timestamp, and evidence.

Do not infer private email addresses from naming patterns or generate guessed addresses.

### 6.2 Contact prioritization

Score contacts using:
- Relevance to an open or likely suitable role.
- Evidence that the contact handles hiring for that role.
- Company and technical-stack fit.
- Recency and reliability of source evidence.
- Whether the candidate has already applied.
- Previous interactions, cooldowns, and suppression status.

Avoid contacting multiple employees at the same company indiscriminately.

### 6.3 Personalized outreach

Use AI to draft individualized messages that:
- Identify the specific role or technical fit.
- Reference only verified public information.
- Highlight one or two relevant candidate accomplishments.
- Explain the candidate's interest in Germany-based opportunities.
- Ask one clear, respectful question or request.
- Keep the message concise, normally around 80–150 words.
- Avoid generic flattery, fabricated familiarity, misleading claims, and pressure tactics.

Do not claim that a candidate has applied unless the application record confirms it.

Support English and German drafts when appropriate. German messages must be grammatically reviewed and must not overstate language proficiency.

### 6.4 Email delivery and legal review

The current outreach send endpoint is fail-closed and Gmail is read-only. Preserve these controls during implementation and testing.

Implement:
- Draft review and approval.
- Recipient and domain suppression.
- Opt-out recording.
- Contact and employer cooldowns.
- Idempotency.
- Duplicate detection.
- Audit logs.
- Provider error handling.
- Rate limits and daily quotas.
- A global emergency stop.

Before enabling cold-email delivery, perform a jurisdiction-specific compliance review. Germany and the EU can impose strict limits on unsolicited commercial email, including B2B messages. A public email address alone does not establish permission to send.

Do not send bulk or deceptive messages. Do not assume consent or a lawful basis. Use approved channels and contact methods appropriate to the recipient and purpose.

Any future email-sending capability must be separately configured and explicitly authorized. Do not add OAuth send scopes, bypass the existing `403` barrier, or silently activate delivery.

### 6.5 Follow-up workflow

Track:
- Drafted.
- Approved.
- Sent, only when provider evidence confirms delivery acceptance.
- Replied.
- Positive reply.
- Referral offered.
- Interview conversation.
- No response.
- Opted out.
- Suppressed.

Generate follow-up recommendations based on actual delivery and response history. Do not follow up with contacts who opted out or whose status is uncertain.

Do not claim an email was sent merely because a draft was approved.

## 7. AI Orchestration and Model Selection

Use Google ADK to orchestrate bounded, auditable tools. Keep business rules deterministic wherever possible.

Recommended logical agents:

1. **Discovery Agent** — finds relevant jobs and official sources.
2. **Verification Agent** — checks freshness and preserves uncertainty.
3. **Qualification Agent** — evaluates role fit against candidate evidence.
4. **Application Preparation Agent** — generates CV and cover-letter drafts.
5. **Application Safety Agent** — validates all eligibility and submission gates.
6. **Recruiter Research Agent** — finds relevant contacts and evidence.
7. **Outreach Draft Agent** — prepares personalized messages.
8. **Outcome Analyst Agent** — tracks conversion, failures, and next actions.

These can be logical sub-agents within the existing service; do not deploy a separate service for each one without demonstrated need.

### Model routing

- Use a cost-effective Flash-class model for extraction, summarization, classification, and first-pass matching.
- Escalate ambiguous or high-value cases to a stronger model.
- Use deterministic validation for required fields, policy checks, quotas, duplicate detection, and submission confirmation.
- Cache reusable company and candidate research.
- Reprocess only when source evidence materially changes.
- Set explicit input/output token limits, timeouts, bounded concurrency, and retry policies.
- Log model, token usage, estimated cost, latency, errors, and task outcome.

Do not treat model-generated confidence as authoritative evidence.

- CAPTCHA, MFA, or anti-bot challenges.
- Unknown submission outcomes.
- Candidate-data mismatches.
- Unexpected external actions.
- Duplicate attempts.
- Budget-limit violations.
- Provider policy or compliance concerns.
